# Whitehash marketing voice

Keep product truth and claim boundaries in `positioning.md`. This file governs how the
verified facts should sound.

- Sound direct, calm, technically credible, and lightly playful.
- Write for people building with, making, collecting, or exploring generative art.
- Lead with the outcome or next action. Let live output, commands, and examples replace
  persuasive adjectives.
- Name Whitehash where it establishes context, aids discovery, or disambiguates a
  package. Once context is clear, describe the capability directly instead of repeating
  “Whitehash does…”.
- Distinguish the art from the software around it. People preserve, display, render, or
  capture art. They build sites, integrations, components, and endpoints. Never imply
  that using the toolkit creates the artwork.
- Write calls to action as a concrete verb plus the mechanism or result: “Use the CLI
  to preserve art”, “Start building with React”, or “Build a capture endpoint”.
- Prefer concrete subjects and technical nouns. Use “The capture engine runs the
  generator in a controlled browser session”, not “It runs in a background browser”.
  Avoid empty openings such as “These are…”.
- Use progressive disclosure. Keep the main line readable, then reveal chain names,
  storage systems, caveats, and configuration details where they become useful.
- Keep “project” and “token” as the canonical product terms. Use “edition” only to
  clarify what one token represents.
- Be welcoming without labeling the reader a beginner, dummy, or nontechnical user.
- Avoid hype words such as “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” and “magic.”
- Avoid em dashes. Use a period, colon, parentheses, or a new sentence.
- Use the logo or one restrained line of wordplay when it carries the brand more cleanly
  than another written product-name mention. Do not force “hash” into ordinary copy.
- Do not imitate a named individual’s style.

## Final review

Confirm that:

- the outcome, mechanism, and next action are concrete;
- the copy does not confuse artwork with the software being built around it;
- the product name appears only where it earns its place;
- technical detail is visible at the right level rather than removed or front-loaded;
- commands, API names, limitations, and claims match the source;
- every sentence still contributes after the proof and examples are present.
