# Whitehash social-card examples

## Approved hero baseline

Use this as the composition and copy-density reference:

```text
Brand: whitehash
Label: Archive CLI
Primary line: Your hash deserves
Secondary line: a better stash.
Support: Paste one fxhash token URL. Keep the art as an offline archive or hostable JSON.
Footer: Generative art. Straight from the source.
```

The primary and secondary lines should each fit on one line at 1280×720. Keep the
complete hook to roughly 4–12 words. Support copy should be one sentence and no more
than two rendered lines.

## Good decisions

- Use the actual hash mark as the dominant visual instead of illustrating an abstract
  archive, blockchain, shield, or folder.
- Reuse the black, warm-white, grey, grid, and blue-glow system from the docs.
- Keep the logo visible in both the top-left lockup and the large hero mark.
- Make a CLI or API card look like the same product, not a separate terminal theme.
- Let code, output structure, or a comparison provide the information on non-hero cards.

## Reject and redo

Reject a card when it:

- could belong to any developer tool after removing the wordmark;
- invents a teal, sepia, gold, purple, or multicolor campaign palette;
- uses a generic AI-generated archive, blockchain, shield, or folder illustration;
- centers the logo in a badge or decorative container;
- shrinks the logo into a footer-only signature;
- uses a documentation screenshot that is unreadable on a phone;
- places more than one headline, one support idea, and one dominant visual on a hero;
- relies on tiny labels or long prose to explain the feature.

Do not accept “technically readable” as visual approval. The card must be recognizably
Whitehash at a glance.
